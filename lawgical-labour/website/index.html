Blog · Labour law updates

What employers need to know

Wages · Effective 1 March 2026

National Minimum Wage increases to R30,23 an hour

On 3 February 2026, the Minister of Employment and Labour announced an increase in South Africa's National Minimum Wage to R30,23 per hour, effective 1 March 2026.

What to do

Check your payroll and employment contracts against the new rate. Contact us for expert labour law advice.

Leave

Annual leave: use it or lose it after 18 months

Any leave balance not taken within 18 months of the leave-earning period is legally forfeited, unless the employee can prove the employer actively blocked them from taking it.

What to do

Keep clear leave records and make sure employees have a fair opportunity to take their leave. Need help with your leave policy? Talk to us.

Dismissals

The Draft Dismissal Code: what you need to know

No major changes, mostly a rewording of existing law. Fair procedure is still required, small businesses still need to follow due process, and a drop in CCMA cases isn't expected.

What to do

Keep following fair procedure for every dismissal. For expert advice and guidance in handling all labour-related matters, contact us.

Compliance · Annual reminder

PAIA: your yearly report to the Information Regulator

Under section 83(4) of PAIA, private bodies are requested to submit their PAIA reports to the Information Regulator each year, via the Regulator's e-Services portal.

What to do

Diarise the annual deadline and confirm the current date with the Information Regulator. Need help? Contact Lawgical Labour for expert assistance with the process.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What if I don’t have a written employment contract?

A written contract is strongly recommended, but its absence does not diminish an employee's rights. The law looks at:

  • Conduct
  • Payslips
  • Policies
  • Emails
  • The reality of the working relationship

Verbal agreements still carry legal weight.

How do I dismiss an employee fairly in South Africa?

You need a fair reason (misconduct, poor performance, ill health, or operational requirements) and a fair procedure. The employee must know the allegations and have a real chance to respond before you decide. Dismissal should be a last resort, in line with the 2025 Code of Good Practice: Dismissal.

How do I run a disciplinary hearing?

Give the employee written notice of the charges and time to prepare, and allow a fellow employee or shop steward to represent them. An impartial chairperson hears both sides, decides on guilt, then considers mitigating factors before choosing a sanction. Confirm the outcome in writing. We help you do this in line with the law.

What is the retrenchment process?

It's the legal process for dismissing employees for operational reasons, such as financial or structural changes. You must give written notice, consult in good faith on alternatives, use fair selection criteria, and pay severance of at least one week's pay per completed year of service.

Can I dismiss an employee for poor performance?

Yes, but only after a fair process. Set clear standards, explain where the employee is falling short, offer training or support, and give reasonable time to improve before considering dismissal.

How should I respond to a CCMA referral?

This is exactly where we can help! But never ignore it, as the matter can go ahead without you. Check whether it was referred on time, gather your records (contract, warnings, hearing minutes), and attend conciliation prepared to discuss settlement. If it isn't resolved, it moves to arbitration.

What must be in an employment contract in South Africa?

The BCEA requires details such as job title, place of work, start date, working hours, pay and deductions, leave, and notice period. Most employers also add probation, confidentiality, and restraint of trade clauses where relevant.
